COMPUTER ETHICS AND CYBER SECURITY

Understanding the Importance of Computer Ethics

Computer ethics refers to the moral principles and standards that guide people in using computers, the internet, and other digital technologies responsibly. As technology becomes part of everyday life, people must understand that their actions online can affect other individuals, organizations, and society.

Computers allow people to communicate, study, work, shop, and access information quickly. However, these advantages also create responsibilities. Users must respect other people's privacy, protect digital information, follow copyright rules, and avoid activities that may cause harm.

Computer ethics helps users distinguish between acceptable and unacceptable behavior when using technology. For example, accessing another person's private account without permission is unethical, even if the information can be easily viewed. Similarly, copying another person's work and presenting it as one's own is a form of dishonesty.

Responsible computer use begins with understanding that actions in the digital world have real consequences. Every user should think carefully before sharing information, downloading files, communicating with others, or accessing digital resources.

Why Computer Ethics Matters

It promotes honesty and responsibility when using technology.

It protects the privacy and rights of other people.

It encourages respect for digital property and original work.

It helps prevent harmful online behavior.

It creates a safer and more respectful digital environment.

Principles of Responsible Computer Use

Responsible computer use means using technology in ways that are safe, honest, respectful, and beneficial. Computer users should follow ethical standards whether they are using a computer for school, work, entertainment, or communication.

One important principle is respect. Users should respect the opinions, privacy, property, and rights of others. They should not use technology to embarrass, threaten, or harm another person.

Another principle is honesty. Users must provide accurate information and acknowledge the original creators of materials they use. Submitting copied assignments, spreading false information, or pretending to be someone else online violates this principle.

Responsibility is also important. Computer users must understand that they are accountable for their online activities. They should protect their accounts, follow school or workplace technology policies, and report suspicious activities when necessary.

Basic Principles

Respect: Treat other users properly and respect their privacy.

Honesty: Do not steal information, copy work, or spread false content.

Responsibility: Be accountable for actions performed using technology.

Fairness: Give others equal opportunities and avoid discrimination.

Safety: Protect devices, accounts, and personal information.

Respect for Ownership: Follow copyright laws and software licenses.

Following these principles helps individuals become responsible digital citizens.

Digital Privacy and Personal Information

Digital privacy refers to a person's ability to control how personal information is collected, used, stored, and shared online. Personal information may include a full name, home address, phone number, email address, school information, photographs, and account details.

Many websites and applications request personal information to provide their services. However, users should understand why the information is being requested and how it may be used. Sharing unnecessary details can increase the risk of privacy problems.

For example, posting a photograph that reveals a home address or sharing a school schedule publicly may expose information that should remain private. Users should carefully check their privacy settings before making information available to others.

Organizations that collect personal information also have a responsibility to protect it. They should use appropriate security measures and handle information according to applicable privacy laws and policies.

Ways to Protect Digital Privacy

Avoid sharing sensitive personal information publicly.

Use privacy settings on social media accounts.

Do not share passwords or verification codes.

Review the permissions requested by applications.

Avoid opening suspicious links or messages.

Log out of accounts when using shared devices.

Think carefully before posting photographs or personal details.

Protecting privacy is a shared responsibility between individual users and organizations.

Copyright, Plagiarism, and Intellectual Property

Intellectual property refers to creations of the mind, including written works, photographs, music, videos, computer programs, designs, and other original materials. These creations are protected by laws and rules that recognize the rights of their creators.

Copyright gives creators certain rights over the use and distribution of their original works. It helps protect their effort, creativity, and ownership. Users should understand that materials available on the internet are not automatically free to copy or distribute.

Plagiarism occurs when someone uses another person's words, ideas, or work without proper acknowledgment and presents them as their own. It is considered academic dishonesty and can damage a person's credibility.

For example, copying an entire article from a website and submitting it as a school assignment without mentioning the source is plagiarism. A responsible student should write information in their own words and provide proper citations when required.

How to Respect Intellectual Property

Give credit to the original author or creator.

Use quotation marks when directly quoting a source.

Follow the rules of the material's license.

Avoid downloading or distributing unauthorized copies.

Use properly licensed images, music, and software.

Cite websites, books, and other references used in assignments.

Respecting intellectual property encourages creativity, fairness, and honesty in the digital community.

Understanding Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities involving computers, digital devices, networks, or the internet. These activities may target individuals, businesses, schools, or government organizations.

As technology continues to develop, cybercrime has become a serious concern. Criminals may attempt to steal personal information, access accounts without permission, distribute harmful software, or deceive people through online communication.

Cybercrime can affect victims financially, emotionally, and socially. For example, a person whose account has been accessed without permission may lose important information or experience unauthorized use of their identity.

Common Types of Cybercrime

Identity Theft: Using another person's personal information without permission.

Phishing: Deceiving users through messages or websites to obtain private information.

Online Fraud: Using false information or dishonest transactions to take advantage of others.

Unauthorized Access: Entering a computer system or account without proper permission.

Cyberbullying: Using digital platforms to harass, threaten, or embarrass another person.

Data Theft: Taking confidential or private information without authorization.

Understanding these forms of cybercrime helps users recognize possible dangers and make safer decisions online.

Common Cybersecurity Threats

Cybersecurity threats are possible dangers that can affect computers, networks, and digital information. These threats may damage files, interrupt services, or expose private data.

One common threat is malware, which refers to software designed to harm a device, interfere with its operation, or access information without proper authorization.

Types of Malware

Virus: A type of malicious program that can attach itself to files and spread when those files are used.

Worm: Malware that can spread between systems without requiring a user to manually share an infected file.

Trojan Horse: A harmful program disguised as legitimate or useful software.

Ransomware: Malware that can prevent users from accessing their files or systems and demand payment.

Spyware: Software that secretly collects information about a user's activities.

Adware: Software that displays unwanted advertisements and may sometimes create privacy or security concerns.

Another common threat is social engineering. It involves manipulating people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that may compromise their security.

Preventive Measures

Install trusted security software.

Keep operating systems and applications updated.

Avoid suspicious downloads and attachments.

Download applications only from trusted sources.

Be careful when receiving unexpected messages.

Back up important files regularly.

Recognizing cybersecurity threats is an important step toward protecting digital devices and information.

Password Security and Authentication

Passwords are commonly used to protect online accounts, computer systems, and personal information. A strong password makes it more difficult for unauthorized individuals to access an account.

A weak password may contain simple words, repeated numbers, or easily guessed personal details. Users should avoid using information such as birthdays, names, or common number combinations as passwords.

It is also important to avoid reusing the same password across multiple accounts. If one account becomes compromised, other accounts using the same password may also be at risk.

Characteristics of a Strong Password

It is sufficiently long.

It uses a combination of different characters.

It does not contain easily guessed personal information.

It is different from passwords used for other accounts.

It is kept private and is not shared with other people.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Multi-factor authentication is a security method that requires more than one form of verification before allowing access to an account. For example, a user may enter a password and then confirm their identity through an authentication application.

MFA provides an additional layer of protection because knowing the password alone may not be enough to access the account.

Users should also keep recovery information updated and never share one-time verification codes with other people.

Safe Internet Browsing and Email Practices

The internet provides access to information, educational resources, entertainment, and communication tools. However, not every website or online message is trustworthy. Users should develop safe browsing habits to avoid suspicious content and possible security problems.

Before visiting a website, users should check whether the address is correct and whether the website appears legitimate. They should be careful with websites that contain unusual messages, unexpected advertisements, or requests for unnecessary personal information.

Email is another common way people communicate and exchange files. Unfortunately, it can also be used to distribute scams, suspicious attachments, and phishing messages.

Safe Browsing Practices

Visit trusted and familiar websites.

Check website addresses carefully.

Avoid clicking suspicious advertisements.

Do not download unknown files.

Keep browsers and applications updated.

Avoid entering personal information on untrusted websites.

Safe Email Practices

Check the sender's email address.

Be cautious of unexpected attachments.

Avoid responding to messages requesting passwords.

Do not trust messages simply because they use official-looking logos.

Verify unusual requests through a separate trusted communication channel.

Report suspicious messages when possible.

Being careful while browsing and checking emails can help prevent many common online security problems.

Social Media Ethics and Responsible Communication

Social media platforms allow people to communicate, share experiences, express opinions, and interact with different communities. Although these platforms provide many benefits, users must follow ethical standards when participating in online discussions.

Digital communication should be respectful and responsible. Users should avoid posting harmful comments, spreading rumors, sharing private photographs without permission, or using social media to embarrass others.

One important concept is netiquette, which refers to proper behavior and manners when communicating online. It encourages users to communicate politely, respect different opinions, and think before posting.

Examples of Good Netiquette

Use respectful language when commenting.

Avoid insulting or threatening other users.

Respect other people's opinions.

Do not share private conversations without permission.

Check information before sharing it.

Give proper credit to original content creators.

Avoid posting content that may harm another person.

Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is the use of digital platforms to repeatedly harass, threaten, embarrass, or intimidate another person. It may happen through social media, messaging applications, online games, and other communication platforms.

People should never use technology to harm others. If cyberbullying occurs, affected individuals should seek help from a trusted adult, teacher, or appropriate authority. They may also use platform reporting and blocking features.

Device and Network Security

Device security involves protecting computers, smartphones, tablets, and other electronic equipment from unauthorized access, harmful software, and data loss.

Every digital device may contain important personal or organizational information. Without proper protection, files and accounts may become vulnerable to security problems.

One basic security measure is keeping software updated. Updates often include improvements, corrections, and security fixes that help protect devices from known vulnerabilities.

Network security focuses on protecting the connections and systems that allow devices to communicate and exchange information. Home networks, school networks, and business networks should be protected against unauthorized access.

Ways to Improve Device Security

Use a password, PIN, or biometric lock on devices.

Install updates when they become available.

Use trusted security applications.

Avoid installing unknown programs.

Lock devices when they are not being used.

Keep important information backed up.

Ways to Improve Network Security

Use a secure Wi-Fi password.

Change default network settings when appropriate.

Avoid sharing network credentials publicly.

Be cautious when connecting to public Wi-Fi.

Use trusted networks when handling sensitive information.

Follow the security policies of schools and organizations.

Device and network security help maintain the confidentiality and availability of digital resources.

Data Protection and Backup Management

Data protection refers to the practices used to keep digital information safe from loss, damage, unauthorized access, and misuse. Data may include documents, photographs, school projects, business records, and other important files.

Losing important data can cause inconvenience and may affect schoolwork, business operations, or personal activities. Data loss may happen because of accidental deletion, device problems, malware, or unexpected events.

One effective way to reduce the impact of data loss is creating regular backups. A backup is an additional copy of important information stored separately from the original files.

Common Backup Methods

External Storage: Saving copies of files on an external hard drive or USB storage device.

Cloud Storage: Keeping copies of files through an online storage service.

Local Backup: Saving additional copies on another storage device within the same location.

Users should choose a backup method that matches their needs and the importance of their information.

Good Backup Practices

Back up important files regularly.

Keep backup copies separate from original files.

Use trusted storage services and devices.

Protect backups with appropriate security settings.

Check whether backup files can be accessed when needed.

Avoid connecting backup devices unnecessarily when a security problem is suspected.

A good backup routine helps users recover important information when unexpected problems occur.

Ethical Use of Information and Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence, commonly known as AI, refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. These tasks may include recognizing patterns, understanding language, generating content, and assisting with problem-solving.

AI tools are increasingly used in education, healthcare, business, communication, and entertainment. They can help users organize information, generate ideas, and understand difficult topics.

However, AI should be used responsibly. Users must remember that AI-generated information may contain errors, incomplete explanations, or misleading statements. Important information should be checked using reliable sources.

In education, AI can support learning by explaining concepts and helping students practice. However, students should not simply submit generated answers as their own work when their school requires original responses.

Responsible AI Use

Use AI as a learning and assistance tool.

Check the accuracy of generated information.

Avoid using AI to spread false or harmful content.

Respect privacy when entering information into AI systems.

Follow school or workplace rules regarding AI use.

Acknowledge AI assistance when required.

Use personal judgment instead of depending entirely on generated answers.

AI is most useful when it supports human understanding, creativity, and responsible decision-making.

Cybersecurity in Schools and Organizations

Cybersecurity is important not only for individuals but also for schools, businesses, government offices, and other organizations. These institutions often store large amounts of information that must be protected.

Schools use digital systems for student records, learning platforms, communication, and administrative activities. Businesses use computer networks to manage customer information, financial records, employee details, and daily operations.

A security problem in an organization may interrupt services, affect productivity, and expose confidential information. Therefore, organizations must establish clear cybersecurity policies and educate their members about safe technology use.

Cybersecurity Responsibilities of Schools

Protect student and teacher information.

Maintain secure learning platforms.

Teach students about online safety.

Limit access to confidential records.

Provide guidelines for responsible computer use.

Cybersecurity Responsibilities of Businesses

Protect customer and employee information.

Use appropriate security controls.

Train employees to recognize suspicious activities.

Maintain secure systems and updated software.

Prepare procedures for handling security incidents.

Acceptable Use Policy

An acceptable use policy is a set of rules that explains how members of an organization are allowed to use its computers, networks, internet connections, and digital resources.

Following these policies helps create a safer and more organized technology environment.

Responding to Cybersecurity Incidents

A cybersecurity incident is an event that may affect the security, confidentiality, integrity, or availability of digital information or systems.

Examples include unauthorized account access, suspicious messages, missing files, malware warnings, and the accidental exposure of confidential information.

Responding properly to an incident is important because careless actions may make the situation worse. Users should avoid guessing, hiding the problem, or attempting actions beyond their knowledge and authority.

Basic Steps When a Security Problem Occurs

Stay Calm: Avoid making rushed decisions.

Stop Suspicious Activity: Do not continue interacting with suspicious links, messages, or files.

Protect the Account or Device: Follow trusted security guidance and seek assistance when necessary.

Report the Incident: Inform a teacher, parent, school administrator, or IT support personnel, depending on the situation.

Preserve Relevant Information: Keep useful details such as warning messages and the time the incident occurred.

Follow Recovery Instructions: Allow authorized personnel to investigate and help restore normal operation.

Organizations may also have incident response procedures that identify who should be contacted, what systems may need attention, and how affected users should be informed.

Reporting a problem early can help reduce its possible effects and prevent similar incidents from happening again.

D. General Summary

Computer ethics and cybersecurity are important parts of responsible technology use. Computer ethics teaches people to respect privacy, intellectual property, and the rights of other users. It encourages honesty, fairness, and accountability in digital activities.

Cybersecurity focuses on protecting devices, networks, accounts, and information from possible threats. Common security risks include malware, phishing, unauthorized access, online fraud, and identity theft. Users can reduce these risks by practicing safe browsing, creating strong passwords, enabling additional authentication, updating software, and backing up important files.

Technology continues to develop, and people must learn how to use it wisely. Every computer user has a responsibility to protect personal information, respect others, and contribute to a safe digital environment.

Final Reminder: Being a responsible computer user means not only knowing how to use technology but also understanding how to use it safely, ethically, and respectfully.
